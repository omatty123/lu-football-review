Long-term weakness

From 2011 through 2021, Lawrence went 17–81. Its average score was 18.4–35.4, a deficit of 16.9 points per game. The best record in that span was only 3–7, in 2016.

A more severe decline

In 2022–2025, the average score became 8.9–49.2. The average deficit grew to 40.4 points. Lawrence was shut out in 15 of 39 games and lost 24 games by at least 30 points.

More than narrow defeats

Only two of the 38 losses in 2022–2025 were by eight points or fewer. Both came in 2024. Most of this period cannot be explained as close games that simply went the wrong way.

Two exceptionally weak seasons, three years apart

The 2022 collapse was followed by a partial scoring recovery, then another collapse in 2025.

2022: 39–511 on the scoreboard

Lawrence scored 3.9 points per game, suffered six shutouts and lost eight times by at least 30 points. The team converted 17.8% of third downs; opponents converted 55.4%. Lawrence threw 11 interceptions and intercepted no opposing passes.

2022 cumulative statistics ↗

2023–2024: improvement without recovery

Scoring rose to 14.6 points per game in 2023 and stood at 12.7 in 2024. Yet those two years produced only one win in 20 games. The 24–10 win over Beloit on October 19, 2024, remains the only victory since 2021.

2023 schedule ↗ · 2024 schedule ↗

2026: better production, still no win

Through six games, Lawrence has scored 65 points and allowed 237: 10.8–39.5 per game. Offensive production has risen to 202.5 yards per game, from 109.8 in 2025.

That is an improvement on the previous season’s figures. It is not yet competitive recovery: the team is 0–6, every defeat has been by at least 10 points, and the average deficit is 28.7 points.

The comparison is provisional. The 2026 schedule is incomplete and includes different opponents. It does not isolate a coaching effect.

Investment and rebuilding promises are part of the record

Roster depth was already a concern in 2015

When Lawrence hired Rob McCarthy, its announcement emphasized recruiting and increasing player numbers. That documents a recognized program problem more than a decade ago. It does not establish roster depth as the sole cause of later results.

2015 hiring announcement ↗

The university documented investment

Lawrence’s 2020 investment statement reports an expansion from three to four full-time football coaches and renovated football offices and locker rooms. It also describes a $5 million Banta Bowl renovation.

2020 athletics investment statement ↗

The facility history describes a $4.5 million fundraising campaign; the investment statement uses $5 million. These are different published descriptions, not two amounts to add together. The facility also serves soccer. Neither figure establishes football-only spending, an annual operating budget, or whether resources matched program needs.

The evidence supports a clear judgment: Lawrence has repeatedly failed to restore competitive football despite coaching changes and documented investment. The six-game 2026 record does not yet overturn that conclusion. Assigning responsibility among recruitment, retention, staffing, resources and institutional priorities requires evidence beyond the scoreboard.

Evidence and calculation notes

This report examines all 16 season schedule pages from 2011 through 2026, including the canceled 2020 season, and all 15 corresponding played-season cumulative-statistics pages. It contains 143 completed games: 137 from completed seasons and six from 2026. It is a dated research snapshot, not an automatically updating feed.

How conflicting source totals were resolved

Game-level schedule scores are the consistent basis for every season total in the report. Thirteen of the 15 cumulative-statistics pages match those scoring totals. Two older pages do not:

  • 2011: the cumulative page reports 211–376; all ten schedule results sum to 231–426. The difference is exactly the 20–50 Ripon result.
  • 2014: the cumulative page reports 107–311; all ten schedule results sum to 135–371. The difference is exactly the combined Beloit (21–13) and Ripon (7–47) results.

The reason for those omissions is not established. We use the complete schedule results and disclose the differences. The initial search copy of the 2026 schedule lacked the Grinnell score; direct retrieval, the 2026 cumulative page and Grinnell’s recap all confirm 7–28.

Definitions, scope and limits
  • Points for / against: final team scores, including points from offense, defense and special teams. Points allowed are not a pure defensive-unit statistic.
  • Per-game averages: point totals divided by completed games. Combined periods are weighted by games, not averages of season averages. Figures round only for display.
  • Shutout suffered: Lawrence scored zero. Large defeat: a loss by 30 or more. One-score loss: a loss by one to eight points; a final-margin measure, not a claim that a game was close throughout.
  • Cancellations: excluded, never treated as defeats. Lawrence’s 2025 schedule marks Knox canceled. No cause for that cancellation is asserted here. The 2020 cancellation announcement documents the pandemic suspension.
  • Historical scope: the official yearly-results archive supports the historical milestones. The detailed game-by-game scoring analysis starts in 2011; this is not a complete statistical history since the program’s founding.
  • Unresolved: comparative budgets, recruiting yields, roster retention, injury rates, admissions policies and peer-adjusted performance were not established. No national ranking or causal explanation is inferred.
